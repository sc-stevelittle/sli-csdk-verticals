The questions we hear most

What are the benefits of digital asset management?

Digital asset management (DAM) software gives organizations one governed place to find and reuse approved content, from images and video to documents and brand files. It cuts duplicate production and enforces rights before content goes live. For enterprises, a DAM also provides the governed foundation AI systems need to represent a brand accurately.

What's the difference between a DAM and a CMS?

A DAM governs digital assets such as images and video, along with the metadata, rights, versions, and approvals that make them reusable. A CMS manages and delivers content for websites and digital experiences, typically drawing on assets from a DAM. In most stacks, assets move between the two manually, which creates version drift. In SitecoreAI, DAM and CMS share a single data model and Intelligent Graph, so approved assets move into experiences without manual transfer and rights are enforced at the point of use.

What should enterprise buyers look for in a DAM?

Enterprise buyers should look for a DAM that handles real operating complexity: multiple brands, regional governance rules, agency access, and regulated content. Core requirements include granular permissions, rights management, configurable taxonomies, and approval workflows that connect to where content is activated. In the AI era, buyers should also ask two questions. Does the DAM structure content so AI systems can use it accurately? Are AI capabilities included, or charged separately by token or seat?

Does putting content in a DAM make it visible to AI?

Not by itself. AI assistants build answers from content they can access and read, and most DAM content is never published anywhere they can reach. Scrunch Knowledge Studio connects approved SitecoreAI DAM content to AI discoverability workflows, so teams can check it against what AI says and use it to close the gaps.

Brand consistency breaks down when governance lives outside the asset, in email chains and manual checks. SitecoreAI DAM attaches permissions, rights, approval status, expiration dates, and usage rules to the asset itself, so governance travels with it wherever it goes.
